TL;DR

At the Edge of Stability, stochastic optimizers do not converge to a single point—they explore a lower-dimensional random pullback attractor. We introduce the Sharpness Dimension (\(\mathrm{SD}\)), a Lyapunov-inspired measure built from the full Hessian spectrum, prove that it bounds the worst-case generalization gap over the attractor, and validate it across MLPs, modular-arithmetic grokking, and 124M-parameter GPT-2.

Abstract

Training modern neural networks often relies on large learning rates, operating at the edge of stability, where the optimization dynamics exhibit oscillatory and chaotic behavior. Empirically, this regime often yields improved generalization performance, yet the underlying mechanism remains poorly understood. In this work, we represent stochastic optimizers as random dynamical systems, which often converge to a fractal attractor set (rather than a point) with a smaller intrinsic dimension. Building on this connection and inspired by Lyapunov dimension theory, we introduce a novel notion of dimension, coined the sharpness dimension, and prove a generalization bound based on this dimension. Our results show that generalization in the chaotic regime depends on the complete Hessian spectrum and the structure of its partial determinants, highlighting a complexity that cannot be captured by the trace or spectral norm considered in prior work. Experiments across various MLPs and transformers validate our theory while also providing new insights into the recently observed phenomenon of grokking.

Geometric intuition (Ellipsoid covering)

One step of \(I - \eta H\) maps a ball \(B(0, R)\) to an ellipsoid with semi-axes \(R\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge R\sigma_d\). Covering it with balls of radius \(R\sigma_{j^*+1}\) turns the volume product \(\prod_{k=1}^{j^*} \sigma_k\) into the partial sum \(S_{j^*}\), giving \(\dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A}\) as the exact scale where \(j\)-volumes stop expanding.

Main Theoretical Result

Modeling a stochastic optimizer as a random dynamical system \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}, \theta, \phi)\) with one-step Jacobian singular values \(\sigma_1(\omega, w) \ge \dots \ge \sigma_d(\omega, w)\), we define the RDS Sharpness of Order \(k\) as \(\lambda_k := \mathbb{E}[\sup_{w \in \mathcal{A}(\omega)} \ln \sigma_k(\omega, w)]\). Letting \(j^* := \max\{ i : \sum_{k=1}^i \lambda_k \ge 0\}\), the Sharpness Dimension is

$$\dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A} := j^* + \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{j^*} \lambda_i}{|\lambda_{j^*+1}|} \quad (1 \le j^* < d),$$

with \(\dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A} = 0\) when \(\lambda_1 < 0\) and \(\dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A} = d\) when \(j^* = d\). Using iterative ellipsoid covering, exterior-power subadditivity, and Birkhoff's ergodic theorem, we prove \(\overline{\dim}_M(\mathcal{A}(\omega)) \le \dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A}\) almost surely, yielding our generalization bound over the entire random pullback attractor \(\mathcal{A}_S(\omega)\):

Theorem 3.4 — Generalization via Sharpness Dimension

Under standard boundedness (\(B\)), \(L\)-Lipschitz continuity, and regular random dynamics assumptions (Assump. B.1–B.3), with probability at least \(1 - \zeta - \gamma\) over \((S, \omega)\), for sufficiently small \(\delta > 0\),

$$\sup_{w \in \mathcal{A}_S(\omega)} \big(\mathcal{R}(w) - \widehat{\mathcal{R}}_S(w)\big) \;\le\; 2L\delta + 2B\sqrt{\frac{4\,\dim_{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{A}_S \,\log(1/\delta)}{n}} + \frac{I_\infty(\mathcal{A}_S(\omega), S) + \log(1/\zeta)}{\sqrt{n}} + \frac{CB^2}{\sqrt{n}}.$$

We also provide a mutual-information-free version via random set stability (Theorem C.6) and a scalable estimator via minibatch Stochastic Lanczos Quadrature (SLQ), reducing memory from \(\Theta(N^2)\) to \(\Theta(mN)\).

Empirical Highlights

Grokking (Mod 97)

On modular addition \((a + b) \bmod 97\), SD achieves up to \(\tau = \mathbf{0.88}\) using only single-checkpoint weights—matching trajectory-based \(E_\alpha\) without storing training trajectories.

In 3-layer ReLU grokking without momentum, classical sharpness and \(\mathrm{tr}(H)\) decay monotonically, while SD rises before grokking and drops sharply across the sudden generalization phase transition.

GPT-2 (124M) on WikiText-2

Trained from scratch across SGD, SGD+Momentum, and AdamW hyperparameter grids, classical sharpness fails and correlates negatively with the loss gap under AdamW (\(\tau = -0.88\)).

Our smoothed SLQ estimators remain consistently positive across all three optimizers, with pseudo-spectrum SD-PS reaching \(\tau \approx \mathbf{0.51}\) (SGD+Mom.) and \(\tau \approx \mathbf{0.69}\) (AdamW).
